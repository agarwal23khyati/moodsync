## IV-E Simulation Study: Shared Mood Context versus Siloed Modules
To estimate the effect of sharing the mood context, 200 simulated users with hidden, mood-dependent tastes interacted with the system for 30 rounds, accepting or rejecting the top outfit, track and recipe with a probability derived from their taste. Three conditions used identical users and random draws: siloed modules that ignore mood, the shared mood context, and the shared context with accept/reject adaptation. The parameter rho sets how far a user's taste departs from the population rules. At rho = 0.5 the shared mood context raised mean acceptance from 0.576 to 0.662 (paired difference +8.6 pp, 95% CI +/-0.6); at rho = 0.75 the difference was +3.9 pp (+/-0.7). Accept/reject adaptation showed no statistically reliable change (-0.5 pp, 95% CI +/-0.5) at rho = 0.5. Siloed modules also require the mood to be entered four times per session instead of once, which is a design property rather than a measured quantity. This is a simulation: the users are generated from the same mood vocabulary as the agents, so part of the shared-context advantage is built in by construction (it shrinks as rho grows); it is evidence about the mechanism, not about real users.
